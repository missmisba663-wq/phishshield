🚩 The 8 red flags

  1. Urgency: "within 24 hours", "final notice".
  2. Threats: account blocked, legal action, arrest.
  3. Requests for secrets: passwords, OTP, PIN, CVV.
  4. Too good to be true: prizes, refunds, job offers.
  5. Mismatched sender: display name ≠ email address; Reply-To differs.
  6. Suspicious links: look-alike domains, IPs, shorteners, odd TLDs.
  7. Unexpected attachments: .html, .zip, .exe, macro documents.
  8. Generic greeting & poor grammar: "Dear Customer".

🔍 How to read a URL

The real owner is the part just before the first single "/", read right to left.

https://paypal.com.secure-verify.xyz/login

Real domain = secure-verify.xyz, not PayPal.

https://accounts.google.com/signin

Real domain = google.com. Legit.

Watch for swapped characters: rn vs m, 0 vs o, 1 vs l, and Unicode look-alikes (xn--).

✅ If you receive a phishing message

  • Don't click, reply, call back or scan.
  • Verify through the official app or a number you look up yourself.
  • Report phishing emails to your IT/security team (or use "Report phishing" in Gmail/Outlook).
  • India: report at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930; report spam SMS on Sanchar Saathi (Chakshu).

🆘 If you already clicked

  • Change the password immediately (and anywhere you reused it).
  • Enable 2-factor authentication (preferably an authenticator app / passkey).
  • If you shared card/bank details, call your bank to block the card now.
  • If money was sent, call 1930 within the "golden hour" to freeze the transfer.
  • Run an antivirus scan if you downloaded anything.
